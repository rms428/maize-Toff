## Faidherbia albida
- **lf = 0.0** (reverse phenology: leafy in dry season, dormant during crop season)
- **Zr = 1500 mm** (vadose zone proxy; true roots reach groundwater at 3–19 m — phreatophyte)
- **Role in model:** facilitation reference archetype; not typical highland Kenya
- **Rainfall range:** semi-arid, 300–800 mm/yr
- **Altitude:** lowland to mid-altitude; riverine/alluvial preference
- **Kenya context:** native to Kenya but more common at lower altitudes and in Sahel; less typical of Laikipia highlands
- Sources:
  - Roupsard et al. (1999) Functional Ecology — isotopic evidence for groundwater use, transpiration ~37 mm/yr at ~7 trees/ha
  - Sarr et al. (2023) BASE — parkland transpiration 0.03–0.20 mm/day plot-level
  - ICRAF Tree Database (Spid=1): https://apps.worldagroforestry.org/treedb2/speciesprofile.php?Spid=1

## Grevillea robusta
- **lf = 1.0** (evergreen year-round)
- **Zr = 800 mm** (roots to 1250 mm in root core study but max density in top 200 mm; effective Zr conservatively 700–900 mm)
- **Role in model:** typical highland Kenya competitor archetype
- **Rainfall range:** 500–2500 mm/yr
- **Altitude:** 1200–2400 m — well suited to Laikipia highlands
- **Kenya context:** most widely planted agroforestry tree in highland Kenya; reduces maize yield 40–50% via water competition in semi-arid conditions
- Sources:
  - **Odhiambo et al. (2001)** Plant and Soil 235:221–233 — root core data to 1250 mm (Machakos, Kenya, 740 mm/yr); Grevillea reduced maize yield ~40%, Gliricidia ~50%; root density highest in top 200 mm for both species; soil moisture measured to 1800 mm. *(Previously miscited as "Ong et al. 2000" — confirmed authorship from PDF)*
  - Long-term productivity I & II, For. Ecol. Mgmt. (2000): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378112700002668

## Alnus acuminata
- **lf = 0.7** (semi-deciduous: sheds some leaves in dry season Aug–Sept, but leafy during most of crop season)
- **Zr = 1000 mm** (moderate depth; N-fixing root nodules concentrated in upper profile)
- **Role in model:** intermediate archetype — some phenological advantage + N-fixation benefit
- **Rainfall range:** 700–2000 mm/yr
- **Altitude:** 1500–3000 m — highland specialist
- **Kenya context:** used in highland agroforestry; less dominant than Grevillea but present
- Sources:
  - Ong et al. (2005) For. Ecol. Mgmt. — Alnus, Grevillea, Paulownia comparison in semi-arid Kenya: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378112705001623
  - ICRAF useful trees database

## Gliricidia sepium (not in overlay — informative comparison)
- **lf ≈ 0.25** (dry-season deciduous: leafless ~3 months, but leafy at start of crop season — brief overlap)
- **Zr ≈ 800 mm** (root cores to 1250 mm; max density in top 200 mm; similar to Grevillea)
- **Role in model:** Would be intermediate lf, similar Zr to Grevillea — but empirically MORE competitive than Grevillea despite lower lf. Suggests lf alone does not determine competition when Zr overlaps fully with crop.
- **Rainfall range:** 600–2500 mm/yr
- **Altitude:** up to 1800 m
- **Kenya context:** Used in alley cropping; Machakos study showed 50% maize yield reduction despite dry-season leaf drop
- Source: Odhiambo et al. (2001) Plant and Soil 235:221–233 — same Machakos, Kenya dataset as Grevillea above

## Croton megalocarpus (candidate — not yet in overlay)
- **lf ≈ 0.9** (broadly evergreen; some leaf drop in harsh dry periods)
- **Zr ≈ 1000–1200 mm** (deep taproot noted; no quantified root depth measurement available)
- **Role in model:** potential intermediate/deep competitor archetype; evergreen but deep-rooted
- **Rainfall range:** 800–1900 mm/yr *(Laikipia ~500–700 mm is on the dry edge)*
- **Altitude:** 1200–2450 m — well suited to Laikipia highlands
- **Kenya context:** native; scattered farmland tree across highland Kenya; valued for fodder, timber, oil
- Source: Orwa et al. (2009) ICRAF Agroforestry Database 4.0 — Croton megalocarpus profile (PDF)
- **Note:** Rainfall range slightly above Laikipia mean; exclude from primary overlay unless validated for drier end

## Notes on literature recency

**Grevillea-specific:** Odhiambo et al. (2001) Plant and Soil 235:221–233 is the primary root/yield citation for Grevillea in Kenya. The Grevillea agroforestry research cluster peaked in the early 2000s; no recent equivalent field study exists. Safe to cite as canonical empirical anchor.

**Recent meta-analyses (useful for broader framing):**
- Coe et al. (2019) *Agronomy for Sustainable Development* — 126-paper meta-analysis, sub-Saharan Africa. Agroforestry generally positive for yield and soil, but no mechanistic decomposition, no HR, no root depth or rainfall effects. DOI: 10.1007/s13593-019-0589-8
- Quandt et al. (2022) *PMC* systematic review — documents that 20% of agroforestry tradeoffs involve competition, recommends right-species-right-place but offers no mechanistic explanation. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9508564/

Both support the paper's framing: outcomes vary but nobody explains *why* in mechanistic terms — that's the gap this model fills.

## Model parameter mapping notes
- `leaf_fraction` = fraction of crop season (180 days) during which tree is leafy
  - lf=0: dormant all season (Faidherbia reverse phenology)
  - lf=1: evergreen (Grevillea)
  - lf=0.7: ~54 days leafless, ~126 days leafy (Alnus)
- `Zr` = effective rooting depth for vadose zone water access [mm]
  - Does NOT capture groundwater access (phreatophyte behavior)
  - Faidherbia Zr=1500 is a vadose zone proxy; real roots go much deeper
- `T_MAX` = 4.0 mm/day for all species in BASE_PARAMS
  - Assumption: same transpiration capacity per unit canopy area as crop
  - Plot-level demand = T_MAX × canopy_cover = 4.0 × 0.2 = 0.8 mm/day
  - `canopy_cover` = 0.2 represents ~15 trees/ha with ~130 m² crown each
